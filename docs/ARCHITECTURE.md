# Opening Trainer: Architecture

Status: **agreed design, v1**. Nothing is implemented yet.

## 1. Goal

A free app for beginners and low-intermediate players (roughly 600–1600) to learn
how to continue an opening against the replies they will actually meet.

You pick your color and the move(s) you want to train, for example "White, 1.e4".
The bot answers with the replies real players choose, and you learn how to react
to each of them. You never need to know opening names, but the app shows them
along the way so you pick them up.

Platforms: **Android app** and **web app**, sharing everything except a thin
platform layer, the same way as MtGLifeTracker.

## 2. Training model

### 2.1 Setup

1. **Color**: White or Black.
2. **Your starting move(s)**: picked on a board, not from a list of names.
   - As White: tap your first move (1.e4, 1.d4, 1.c4, 1.Nf3, 1.e3, …). Each
     option shows how often it is played, so beginners see what is common.
   - As Black: tap your first move (for example ...e5 or ...c5). White's first
     move is then either
     - **random**: chosen among White first moves after which your reply is a
       book move (so ...c5 gets 1.e4, ...d5 gets 1.d4, 1.c4 or 1.Nf3), weighted
       by how often they are played, or
     - **picked by you**: you tap White's first move too (for example 1.e4).

     The picker only offers White first moves after which your reply is book.
   - You can extend the line further if you want (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3), but one move
     is enough.
3. **Opponent**:
   - **Random** (default): the bot picks its replies by how often real players
     choose them.
   - **Specific**: you also tap the opponent's reply you want to train against
     (after 1.e4: 1...c5, 1...e5, 1...c6, …). From there on the bot again plays
     realistic moves.
4. **Options**:
   - Session length: 5–10 of your own moves.
   - Feedback: after every move, or once at the end.
   - "Show best move" hint button.
   - Stockfish eval bar.

The setup is therefore just a **fixed starting line** (made of your moves and
optionally the opponent's) plus options. Whatever comes after the starting line is
what you train. A named opening such as "Italian Game" is simply a longer
starting line, so it needs no special handling.

### 2.2 During a session

- The board starts after the fixed line (with that line shown in the move list).
- **Session length counts from the end of the starting line**: "8 moves" means
  8 of your own moves after it.
- The bot plays a book reply (see 4.3). When the book runs out, the bot continues
  with Stockfish moves until the session ends.
- **Instant feedback mode**: after each move you see:
  - "Book ✓" with a short explanation, or
  - "Not book", with the usual move(s) and a plain-language reason. You can then
    choose **Try again** or **Continue anyway**.
- **End feedback mode**: you play all your moves uninterrupted, then get a
  move-by-move report.
- **Hint**: arrows for the book move(s) and Stockfish's best move. Hints used are
  marked in the report.
- **Eval bar**: Stockfish evaluation of the current position, updated after
  every move.
- **Undo**: on your turn you can take back your last move together with the
  bot's reply, as often as you like, back to the end of the starting line. Works
  in both feedback modes. After an undo the bot chooses its reply afresh, so the
  same move can meet a different answer. The report grades the line you finally
  played and shows how many takebacks you used per move.

### 2.3 Feedback for beginners

Feedback should teach, not only grade. Each move gets:

- **A verdict**: Book / Playable / Inaccuracy / Mistake / Blunder. Non-book moves
  are graded by how much evaluation they lose against Stockfish's best move.
- **A short reason**, generated by rules in `core` (no AI). Examples: "develops a
  piece", "fights for the center", "castles your king to safety", "moves the same
  piece twice", "blocks your bishop", "brings the queen out early", "leaves the
  pawn on e5 undefended".
- **The opening name** once the position matches a named opening ("You're in the
  Italian Game").

The tone should be friendly and a bit playful. Streaks and achievements come later
(see 9).

## 3. Modules

```
ChessOpenings/
├── chess/     Rules engine. Pure Kotlin, no dependencies.
├── core/      Opening book, training session logic, feedback,
│              TrainerController, and the interfaces platforms implement.
│              Pure Kotlin + kotlinx-coroutines.
├── ui/        Compose screens shared by Android and web: board, setup,
│              training, report. Talks to core only through TrainerState
│              and TrainerAction.
├── app/       Android app: native Stockfish engine, SharedPreferences store,
│              AndroidPlatform.
├── web/       Browser app: standalone Gradle build on Kotlin/Wasm, like
│              MtGLifeTracker's web/. Compiles chess, core and ui from source;
│              adds Stockfish WASM in a Web Worker and localStorage.
└── tools/
    └── bookgen/  JVM command-line tool that builds the opening book from
                  game data. Uses chess/. Not shipped in the apps.
```

Dependency direction: `chess` ← `core` ← `ui` ← `app` / `web`, and `chess` ←
`tools/bookgen`. Nothing in `chess` or `core` knows about Android, the browser or
Compose. Both are unit-tested on the JVM.

`chess` is a separate module so that `bookgen` can reuse the exact same move
generation and position hashing as the apps.

## 4. Opening book

### 4.1 Source data

Real games from the **Lichess open database** (database.lichess.org, CC0): monthly
dumps of all rated games in PGN, compressed with zstd.

`bookgen` only reads the first 20 plies of each game, and uses two rating bands:

| Band | Games | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | both players 2000+ | Which moves count as book for *you* |
| **Club** | average 1200–1800 | How often the *bot* plays each reply: what you will really face |

Your recommended moves come from strong players; the opponent's replies come
from players around your level.

Opening names come from the `lichess-org/chess-openings` list (CC0) and are
attached to positions by hash.

### 4.2 Building the book

`bookgen` runs these steps:

1. **Count**: stream one or more monthly dumps (`curl | zstd -d | bookgen count`),
   play each game's first 24 plies with `chess` (enough for a starting line plus
   10 moves), and count games per
   (position hash, move, band). Transpositions merge automatically because
   positions are keyed by hash.
   Memory is bounded by lossy counting: past 2 million positions, the rarest are
   dropped; popular positions keep exact counts.
2. **Prune**: a position needs 100+ strong games before its moves can be user book,
   and 200+ club games before the bot takes replies from it. Moves need a 5%
   (strong) or 3% (club) share.
3. **Evaluate**: one Stockfish search per position (depth 16 by default) scores
   the engine's best move together with the popular moves, so all scores come from
   the same search. (Separate searches differ by search noise, which showed up as
   false losses in testing.) The Lichess cloud-evaluation dump could replace part
   of this step later.
4. **Classify** each move:
   - **Book for the user**: played by strong players (share ≥ 5%) and losing at
     most ~40 centipawns against the best move.
   - **Bot candidate**: played by club players (share ≥ 3%) and losing at most
     ~80 centipawns. This gives "theory plus common, not-too-bad move orders".
   - All thresholds are parameters of `bookgen`.
5. **Write** `book.bin`.

The thresholds will need tuning once we see real numbers.

**Where it runs:** the manual **Build opening book** GitHub Actions workflow
(`.github/workflows/book.yml`) streams a month, builds `data/book.bin`, uploads
the counts and a dump of the main lines, and pushes the book to a `book/<run>`
branch for review. `bookgen` also runs locally; see `tools/bookgen/README.md`.

A full month is about 30 GB compressed. Streaming avoids storing it, and we stop
after enough games per band (a few million is plenty for 20 plies).

### 4.3 Bot move choice

- From the current position, take the bot candidates and choose one at random,
  weighted by club-band game counts.
- In specific-opponent mode, the bot's first reply after your starting line is
  fixed; after that it is random as above.
- As Black with a random White first move: White's first move is drawn from the
  White first moves after which the user's chosen reply is "book for the user",
  weighted by club-band counts.
- No candidates left (out of book): Stockfish's best move. Full strength, no
  handicap, at the same search depth on both platforms.

### 4.4 File format

`book.bin` is one binary file shared by both apps, loaded from resources:

- **Header**: magic, version, thresholds used.
- **Positions**: sorted by 64-bit key, binary-searched at runtime.
- **Each move**:
  - move (16 bits),
  - strong-band and club-band counts (scaled),
  - evaluation loss in centipawns,
  - flags (user book, bot candidate).
- **Name table**: position key → opening name.

Position keys use **Polyglot-compatible Zobrist hashing**. Its published test
vectors give us free correctness tests, and we can compare against existing
Polyglot books while debugging.

Expected size: a few MB. To be confirmed after the first real build.

## 5. `chess`: rules engine

Our own implementation, written for clarity over speed. We need legal moves for a
handful of positions per second in the apps, and a few hundred million moves in
`bookgen`, which is fine on the JVM.

- **Board**: 64-square mailbox. Bitboards aren't needed for this workload.
- **Position**: immutable. `legalMoves()`, `play(move): Position`, check,
  checkmate, stalemate, castling rights, en passant.
- **Move**: packed `Int` (from, to, promotion, flags).
- **Formats**: FEN in and out, SAN in and out (PGN parsing in `bookgen`, display
  in the apps), UCI in and out (Stockfish).
- **Hashing**: Polyglot Zobrist key.

Tests:

- **Perft** on the standard suite (start position, "Kiwipete", positions 3–6) to
  published node counts. This catches nearly every move-generation bug.
- **SAN round trips**, including disambiguation, promotions, castling, check and
  mate suffixes.
- **Polyglot key** test vectors from the Polyglot specification.

## 6. `core`: training logic

### 6.1 Interfaces implemented by the platforms

```kotlin
interface Engine {
    /** Best [multiPv] lines for [fen] to [depth], optionally restricted to [searchMoves]. */
    suspend fun analyze(fen: String, depth: Int, searchMoves: List<String> = emptyList(), multiPv: Int = 1): List<EngineLine>
}
```

Platforms also load `book.bin` (Android assets, a fetch on the web) and store settings;
both are plain app code around `core`. Later: a `ProgressStore` for streaks and
achievements.

### 6.2 The contract between screens and logic

The screens only read `TrainerState` and send `TrainerAction` to `TrainerController`
(`core/training`). All rules, timing (bot delay) and engine calls live there.

```kotlin
class TrainerController(book: OpeningBook, engine: Engine, scope: CoroutineScope, …) {
    val state: StateFlow<TrainerState>
    fun dispatch(action: TrainerAction)
}
```

- **`TrainerState`**: phase, position and orientation, last move, tap-to-move
  selection and targets, pending promotion, move list (with verdicts, takebacks,
  hints used), feedback, hint and arrows, eval (White's view), opening name, progress
  (moves played and total), whether undo is possible, report, error.
- **`TrainerAction`**: `Start(setup)`, `SquareTapped`, `PieceDropped`,
  `PromotionChosen`, `PromotionCancelled`, `ShowHint`, `Retry`, `Continue`, `Undo`,
  `Restart`, `ResumeFrom(moveIndex)` (from the report: back to before one of your
  moves, to try something else), `Quit`.
- **Setup**: `TrainingSetup(color, line, randomWhiteFirstMove, options)`. The setup
  screen asks `SetupCatalog` for the choices: the user's moves, the opponent's
  replies, Black's first moves, and the White first moves that fit a Black reply.
  Each choice comes with SAN, popularity share and opening name.
- **Feedback**: `Verdict(quality, bookMoves, bestMove, lossCp, reasons)`. Reasons are
  enums (`DEVELOPS_PIECE`, `EARLY_QUEEN`, `BLOCKS_BISHOP`, `HANGS_PIECE`, …); `ui`
  turns them into friendly text.

`ui` wraps the controller in a small androidx `ViewModel` so it survives configuration
changes on Android.

### 6.3 Session state machine

```
Start → BOT_THINKING (bot to move) | YOUR_MOVE
YOUR_MOVE → move → CHECKING (instant mode) → book: feedback note → BOT_THINKING | ANALYZING
                                           → not book: FEEDBACK → Retry → YOUR_MOVE
                                                                → Continue → BOT_THINKING | ANALYZING
YOUR_MOVE → move (end mode) → BOT_THINKING | ANALYZING
YOUR_MOVE → Undo → YOUR_MOVE (your last move and the bot's reply taken back)
BOT_THINKING → YOUR_MOVE | ANALYZING (session length reached, or game over)
ANALYZING → REPORT
```

**Grading.** A book move is always "Book" and needs no engine. Any other move is
graded by its loss against the engine's best move: ≤30 cp Playable, ≤90 Inaccuracy,
≤200 Mistake, otherwise Blunder. The best move and the played move are scored in one
search, as in `bookgen`.

**Feedback.** In instant mode, a non-book move where book moves exist always stops
for Retry or Continue. Past the end of the book, only inaccuracies and worse stop the
user.

**Bot.** The bot plays a bot candidate weighted by club games. When there is none, it
plays Stockfish's best move.

## 7. Engines (platform)

| | Android | Web |
|---|---|---|
| Binary | Stockfish built from source with the NDK for arm64-v8a, armeabi-v7a, x86_64 | Stockfish.js "lite single-threaded" WASM build |
| Packaging | Shipped as `libstockfish.so` in `jniLibs`, so Android allows executing it from the native library directory | Static file served next to the app |
| Talks via | UCI over the process's stdin/stdout | UCI over Web Worker messages; small JS interop file, like MtGLifeTracker's Firebase interop |
| Network file | Small NNUE net only, to keep the APK small | Embedded in the lite build (~7 MB) |

Both sit behind `Engine`; one engine instance per app, with requests queued.

**Risks to check first with a small spike:**

- The Android packaging and process approach.
- APK size with the NNUE network.

## 8. `ui`: shared Compose screens

- **Board**: drawn with Compose Canvas. Drag and tap-to-move, arrows, highlights,
  promotion picker. Pieces: the classic Lichess "cburnett" set (GPLv2+), turned into Compose vector
  images by `tools/pieces/generate_pieces.py`. Board: light blue and darker blue squares.
- **Screens**: Setup (color, starting-line picker board with move popularity,
  opponent, options), Training (board, eval bar, feedback card, hint, move list),
  Report.
- **Platform interface**, like MtGLifeTracker: back handling, share, keep screen
  on, and anything else that differs. Text lives in `Strings.kt`.
- The web build needs WebAssembly GC: current Chrome, Firefox, and
  Safari 18.2+.

## 9. Later

- **Progress**: streaks, per-line success rate, achievements ("10 book games in a
  row", "survived the Sicilian"), spaced repetition of lines you get wrong. All
  behind `ProgressStore`, local only at first.
- Opponent level setting (rating band for the bot's replies). The book would carry
  more bands.
- More explanation rules, possibly per-opening tips.

## 10. Testing

Every module below `ui` is unit-tested on the JVM; tests run in CI on every push.

- **`chess`**:
  - perft node counts for the standard test positions,
  - FEN, SAN and UCI round trips, including edge cases (disambiguation,
    promotions, castling through check, en passant pins),
  - Polyglot keys against the published test vectors,
  - a fixture of random games generated with python-chess, used as an oracle:
    every position's FEN, legal-move count, SAN of each move and Polyglot key
    must match.
- **`core`**:
  - book reader against a small hand-written `book.bin`,
  - bot move choice (weights, specific opponent, White's random first move for
    Black),
  - session state machine with a fake `Engine` and a fixed random seed: every
    transition, including retry, continue, undo and session end,
  - grading thresholds,
  - each explanation rule on hand-picked positions.
- **`bookgen`**: count, prune, classify and write steps on a few small PGN files,
  and a read-back check of the written file.
- **`ui`**: Compose UI tests for the board (tap-to-move, drag, promotion) and the
  screens' reaction to given `TrainerState`s, run on the JVM like MtGLifeTracker's.
- **Platform engines**: a small instrumented test per platform that the engine
  starts and answers a UCI search.

## 11. Build, CI, release

- Gradle with Kotlin and Compose Multiplatform versions aligned with
  MtGLifeTracker (Kotlin 2.4.20, Compose 1.12.1).
- **CI** on pushes and PRs:
  - `chess` and `core` unit tests (perft included),
  - Android build and lint,
  - web build.
- **Pages**: deploys the web app on pushes to `main`.
- **Book**: built by the manual `bookgen` workflow. The resulting `book.bin` is
  committed via PR (a few MB), so app builds don't depend on Lichess being
  reachable.

## 13. Decisions

1. **As Black** you pick your own first move. White's first move is random among
   those that allow it as a book reply, or picked by you (see 2.1).
2. **Session length** counts from the end of the starting line.
3. **Out of book**, the bot plays full-strength Stockfish.
4. **Package**: `com.kregosh.chessopenings`.

## 14. Suggested build order

Each step is reviewable on its own.

1. **`chess`** with perft, SAN and Polyglot tests.
2. **`bookgen`** and its GitHub Actions workflow, producing the first real
   `book.bin`. We look at the numbers and tune thresholds.
3. **`core`**: book reader, session state machine, grading, explanation rules,
   with tests.
4. **Engine spike** on Android and web.
5. **`ui`** plus the `app` and `web` shells.
6. **CI, Pages deploy, Android release workflow.**
